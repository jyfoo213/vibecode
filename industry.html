FOLLOW THE VALUE CHAIN

AI is a stack. Nobody builds it alone.

01 / COMPUTE

Start with the physical world

Chip equipment enables fabrication. Foundries manufacture chips. Designers turn those chips into accelerators for AI workloads.

02 / INTELLIGENCE

Compute becomes capability

Cloud providers operate compute infrastructure. Model labs use it to train and serve systems that generate text, images, and code.

03 / PRODUCTS

Capability finds a customer

Data platforms and applications connect models to useful work. Money, distribution, and feedback flow back through the stack.

FROM THE REEL · THE “AI BUBBLE” MAP

The loop, step by step.

Money goes in as investment, comes back as compute revenue, and the same companies sell the models. Customers, investors, distributors and competitors — all at once. Map total shown on screen: $26 trillion.

  1. Investors fund the labs — Microsoft holds roughly 27% of OpenAI; NVIDIA puts $30 billion into OpenAI; SoftBank, OpenAI’s biggest outside investor, builds the data centre OpenAI leases through SB Energy; Amazon and Microsoft invest in Anthropic; AMD agrees up to $5 billion into Anthropic; NVIDIA invests in Anthropic; Google holds a large Anthropic stake, keeps investing, and backs $5 billion of loans Anthropic uses to lease data centres.
  2. Labs spend it back on compute — OpenAI buys NVIDIA chips in bulk, rents Oracle capacity, and uses Microsoft, Amazon and Google clouds plus CoreWeave; Anthropic runs on Google Cloud and commits $100 billion across Microsoft and Amazon clouds combined (split not stated).
  3. Suppliers book it and reinvest — Oracle buys tens of billions in NVIDIA chips for its data centres; NVIDIA invests in CoreWeave and $5 billion in Intel, buys CoreWeave’s unused capacity, and guarantees OpenAI’s SB Energy lease; CoreWeave stocks its centres with NVIDIA GPUs; OpenAI holds $350 million of CoreWeave stock.
  4. The same companies distribute the models — the clouds sell compute to the labs and sell the labs’ models onward, while building rival models of their own; Google sells compute to OpenAI while holding Anthropic; Meta buys capacity from Google Cloud and CoreWeave while running its own AI labs.
  5. Everyone competes while cooperating — that is the reel’s punchline: a customer is also a distribution partner, an investor, and a competitor.
  6. The reel’s question — the revenue is real, but is it enough to cover hundreds of billions in future commitments? If one company fails, the shock runs through the whole loop.

FROM THE NEWS · MARVELL–LUMENTUM

Marvell buys Lumentum.

Marvell Technology is buying optical-components maker Lumentum in an all-stock deal. Terms as stated in the segment: 4.5 Marvell shares per Lumentum share, about $1,132 per share, a 23.9% premium to the last price before talks were rumoured on 28 October. Shares that morning, as stated: Marvell up about 3.3%, Lumentum up 2.5%.

Why, as framed in the segment: Marvell is betting big-tech AI spending stays high, keeping demand up for the optical components that move data at high speeds. The post’s written caption adds two things the spoken segment did not: a $115 billion deal value, and the framing that this steps up Marvell’s challenge to Broadcom.

Also from the segment, citing reports: NVIDIA invested $2 billion in each company earlier in 2026 — no further detail given. No closing date or completion terms were stated. The exact cents on the per-share price were unclear, so this section says about $1,132.

What changes on the map: two new silicon-layer players (Marvell, Lumentum) and three new dashed lines — the acquisition plus the two reported NVIDIA investments. The two new profiles are reel-sourced, not primary-product-reviewed; they are the exception to the notice at the top of the page.

Source: CNBC reel, 2 Nov 2026. Paraphrased, not transcribed.
