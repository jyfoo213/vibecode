THE AI PM FIELD GUIDE

PRACTICE / DECISIONS, NOT BUZZWORDS

Speak like a PM.
Reason about AI.

Practice six realistic scenarios. Write your answer before opening the illustrative response, then compare it against the rubric.

These are AI-generated practice prompts and illustrative answers, not questions you personally asked or claims about your career. Your drafts stay in this browser; export them to share. No automatic interview score.

Start with the workflow and error cost.

Design a support copilot

A support team wants to cut handling time. Would you build an autonomous reply agent?

Compare with an illustrative answer

Clarify user segments, ticket types, baseline time and error severity. Pilot draft assistance with source passages and human send approval. Evaluate policy support and completeness before measuring handling time and resolution. Expand automation only where evidence and risk permit.

Review criteria

  • Defines buyer, user and workflow baseline.
  • Chooses draft vs autonomous behavior based on risk.
  • Separates offline quality from live time and resolution metrics.
  • Defines escalation, permissions and rollback.
Translate disagreements into a calibrated rubric.

Own the evaluation

The demo looks excellent, but reviewers disagree about response quality. Who owns the eval?

Compare with an illustrative answer

The PM owns the product success definition with domain and engineering partners. Inspect disagreements, segment failure types, and calibrate reviewers on concrete examples. Keep ambiguous cases visible, reserve held-out examples, and track costly failures separately from average scores.

Review criteria

  • Names accountable and contributing roles.
  • Uses examples rather than a vague quality score.
  • Calibrates subjective criteria and captures uncertainty.
  • Connects the release gate to user impact.
Treat benchmarks as shortlist evidence.

Choose a model on evidence

A frontier model scores higher on coding benchmarks. Should your research assistant switch?

Compare with an illustrative answer

Test the candidate against the current system on representative research tasks under equal tools and budgets. Measure evidence support, completeness, latency and cost per success. Include migration and reliability constraints, and keep the current version as a rollback option.

Review criteria

  • Explains why coding scores do not establish research quality.
  • Controls model, harness and budget differences.
  • Measures relevant task segments and costs.
  • Defines release and rollback criteria.
Trace the workload before changing prices.

Reduce cost without hiding failures

Your AI feature costs twice the forecast. What do you investigate first?

Compare with an illustrative answer

Inspect call counts, growing context, output/reasoning tokens, retries, cache misses and tool fees. Find the largest contributor. Test fewer sequential calls or shorter useful output against a quality floor; compare total cost per successful task, not token price alone.

Review criteria

  • Uses usage traces rather than guessing.
  • Includes retries and non-model costs.
  • Changes a measurable bottleneck.
  • Protects a stated quality floor.
Decompose the evidence path.

Debug a grounded answer

The assistant cites a policy page but gives the wrong refund rule. Is this a model problem?

Compare with an illustrative answer

Check document freshness and permissions, retrieval recall, ranking, selected passage and whether the claim is supported. Label the failing stage and add an eval. Retrieval changes may solve the problem without a new model or weight training.

Review criteria

  • Does not equate a citation with support.
  • Separates retrieval and generation errors.
  • Tests freshness and unanswerable queries.
  • Proposes a narrow experiment and regression case.
Show the decisions and evidence, not invented credentials.

Tell a credible transition story

You have PM experience but no model-training job. How do you demonstrate AI PM readiness?

Compare with an illustrative answer

Choose a real workflow you understand, document the baseline and users, and build a bounded prototype. Include an eval set, failure analysis, cost model, permissions and a pilot plan. Describe exactly what you did and what remains untested. Transfer domain judgment without claiming unsupported production impact.

Review criteria

  • Connects prior PM strengths to an AI product decision.
  • Includes inspectable artifacts.
  • Distinguishes prototype evidence from production results.
  • Explains a trade-off and a lesson from failure.

QUESTION BANK · OPENAI & ANTHROPIC HIRING PROCESS

Thirty-two questions, answered with a framework.

From a hiring-process infographic, not from either company's careers page. Answer aloud before opening a tip. Each tip gives a structure plus what the company weights — not a script.

Source: Malay Krishna (PM Coach) LinkedIn infographic, via a screenshot shared 2 Nov 2026. Compensation figures are the infographic's — OpenAI $255K–$325K + equity, Anthropic $285,000–$305,000 — indicative only, not verified, and not tied to a specific role. Live bands for real seats vary widely. What the two companies weight differently, per this infographic: OpenAI leans on mission alignment, product sense and speed under ambiguity; Anthropic digs deeper on decision reasoning and adds a live coding screen even for a PM role, testing technical fluency.

OpenAI · Behavioural

What are your past experiences as a PM?

Tell it as an arc, not a résumé read-out. One line per chapter, each ending in a decision you owned and a result. Land on why the next chapter is AI product — the through-line matters more than the list.

Tell me about a product you previously launched.

Use situation → riskiest assumption → launch plan → metric → what you changed after launch. Name the assumption the launch actually tested; interviewers remember the test, not the feature list.

Tell me about a test you've done.

One test, fully traced: hypothesis, setup, primary metric, guardrail metric, result, decision. If the test failed, say what it killed — a clean kill is a good answer.

If you had to do this project faster, what would you have done differently?

Name what you would cut (scope, not quality), what you would run in parallel, and which decision you would make earlier. Show the trade-off you accepted — speed always costs something.

How do you handle working in highly ambiguous environments?

Show a system, not a temperament: a written problem statement, the smallest testable slice, a decision log, and a weekly re-framing. Ambiguity is a sequencing problem — what do you resolve first?

How do you process and grow from past experiences?

Show a post-mortem habit: what happened, what you misjudged, what changed in your checklist afterwards. One concrete changed behaviour beats a general claim about learning.

How do you overcome difficult situations?

Pick a conflict or a hard constraint, and show the conversation, not just the outcome. End with the relationship intact or the lesson priced into the next plan.

How do you get stuff done and prioritize projects?

Show the queue and the cut line: a framework (impact vs effort, or RICE), one example of something you deprioritised, and what happened to it. Prioritisation is visible only in what you said no to.

Why are you interested in working at OpenAI?

Be specific, not flattering: the mission plus one product direction you want to work on — agents, enterprise, evals — tied to a problem you have actually wrestled with.

What do you like about OpenAI?

Name a product decision, not a vibe: for example, shipping agentic capability with evals and guardrails attached, or the pace from research to product. Say why it matches how you think.

What don't you like about OpenAI?

Honest, small, forward-looking: a tension you would want to explore, such as pace versus safety communication, or consumer polish versus enterprise depth. Frame it as a question, not a complaint.

How do you align your work with the mission and values of OpenAI?

Translate mission into a decision: a time you chose the safer or slower path, or broader access over a narrow win. Values show in trade-offs, not slogans.

OpenAI · Product sense

What goal would you set for an AI-only social network that OpenAI is building?

First pick the job: companionship, creation, or discovery. Then a north-star metric that fits it — weekly meaningful conversations, or creations per active user — plus the guardrails: identity, authenticity, and safety are the product problem when everyone on the network is AI-mediated.

What industry could benefit most from enterprise ChatGPT?

Pick one and defend it: knowledge work plus documents plus regulated workflows plus measurable time saved. Professional services, healthcare operations, and financial services all fit — but name the deployment wedge: pilot, evals on real work, then rollout.

What's your experience with AI-driven products?

Structure it as system, not model: what you built or used, what the model did versus the system around it (retrieval, evals, guardrails), and what broke. The point is showing you think in systems.

What's your favorite product and how would you improve it?

Pick one you use daily. One friction, one improvement framed as hypothesis plus metric plus risk. Resist redesigning everything — a single sharp change beats a laundry list.

Design an umbrella for kids.

Clarify the split first: the user is the child, the buyer is the parent. Constraints follow: safety, size, weight, durability. Then delight: light, colour, easy open. Success metric: the parent repurchases, or the child actually uses it — pick one and say why.

OpenAI · Analytical & metrics

Estimate the number of ChatGPT users worldwide.

Structure beats the number: segment (free, paid, enterprise and education, API users), triangulate from public signals, state every assumption, sanity-check the total. Say what would make your estimate wrong.

How would you measure success for OpenAI? What if instrumentation went down?

Separate the north star (weekly users doing valuable work, retained developers) from input metrics (latency, quality, cost per successful task). For the outage: agree the degraded-mode dashboard in advance — sampled logs, support tickets, billing signals, delayed batch. Never fly blind; say what you would stop deciding until data returns.

What topline metric goal would you set for Airbnb?

One topline — nights booked, or gross booking value — defended with the health metrics beneath it: guest satisfaction, host retention, cancellation rate. The counter-metrics are the answer; they are what stops the topline being gamed.

Anthropic · Recruiter screening

Tell me about yourself.

Two minutes, three beats: present, past, future. End on why this role is the next step, not a random walk. Keep it under two minutes — the recruiter is screening for coherence.

Why Anthropic specifically?

Be concrete: the safety-first research posture, Constitutional AI as an approach, Claude's enterprise positioning, long-context and agent work. Show you have used the product — a generic answer here is the fastest rejection.

Why this role?

Map the role's core problem to your strongest evidence, then name the gap you are excited to close. The shape is: this problem, this proof, this growth.

Walk me through your resume.

Same arc as “tell me about yourself”, but hit the decision points: why each move, what you owned, what changed because of you. Moves without reasons read as drift.

What are your career goals? Where do you want to go in 3–5 years?

Give direction, not a title: for example, leading AI products where trust and safety are the moat. Tie it to what Anthropic builds, so the future points at this company rather than past it.

Anthropic · Hiring manager screening

When you describe a project, they dig deeper. These four follow-ups are the drill — prepare every story to survive them.

Why did you make that decision?

State the options, the deciding evidence, and the constraint that forced the choice. No post-hoc rationalisation: name what you did not know at the time.

What were the tradeoffs?

Name what you gave up explicitly, and who paid for it. A tradeoff without a loser is not a tradeoff — if nobody lost, you have not found the real cost yet.

What would you do differently now?

Show the updated model: what new evidence changed your mind. Do not trash your past self; show the learning as a changed rule, not a regret.

Did the solution work, and how do you know?

Metric plus counter-metric plus time window. Then separate outcome from luck: would you make the same call again with the same information? That is the question behind the question.

Anthropic · Live coding interview

Unusual for a PM role, and that is the point: per this infographic, Anthropic tests technical fluency directly. Think aloud throughout — they are grading reasoning, not syntax.

Parse all URLs from a list and count matches for a given domain (with follow-ups about asynchronous design and scaling).

Clarify first: input format, scale, malformed URLs. Start with a simple correct pass, then discuss the follow-ups: streaming and batching for async, sharding and approximate counting at scale. Say what breaks first as volume grows.

Scale a token-generating function to handle 100,000 requests per second (testing throughput, distributed latency, and pragmatic design).

Back-of-envelope first: tokens per second against a latency budget. Then the bottlenecks in order: accelerator throughput, KV cache, batching, queueing. Pragmatic design means naming degradation explicitly — rate limits, caching, async queues — not pretending infinite scale.

Implement a simple key-value store with evolving constraints around persistence and compression.

Start minimal: in-memory map plus a write-ahead log. Add compaction only when the constraint demands it. Evolving constraints are a test of re-design: ask what changed, adjust the design, and state the durability-versus-speed trade-off at each step. Do not over-build upfront.
