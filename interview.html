THE AI PM FIELD GUIDE

PRACTICE / DECISIONS, NOT BUZZWORDS

Speak like a PM.
Reason about AI.

Practice six realistic scenarios. Write your answer before opening the illustrative response, then compare it against the rubric.

These are AI-generated practice prompts and illustrative answers, not questions you personally asked or claims about your career. Your drafts stay in this browser; export them to share. No automatic interview score.

Start with the workflow and error cost.

Design a support copilot

A support team wants to cut handling time. Would you build an autonomous reply agent?

Compare with an illustrative answer

Clarify user segments, ticket types, baseline time and error severity. Pilot draft assistance with source passages and human send approval. Evaluate policy support and completeness before measuring handling time and resolution. Expand automation only where evidence and risk permit.

Review criteria

  • Defines buyer, user and workflow baseline.
  • Chooses draft vs autonomous behavior based on risk.
  • Separates offline quality from live time and resolution metrics.
  • Defines escalation, permissions and rollback.
Translate disagreements into a calibrated rubric.

Own the evaluation

The demo looks excellent, but reviewers disagree about response quality. Who owns the eval?

Compare with an illustrative answer

The PM owns the product success definition with domain and engineering partners. Inspect disagreements, segment failure types, and calibrate reviewers on concrete examples. Keep ambiguous cases visible, reserve held-out examples, and track costly failures separately from average scores.

Review criteria

  • Names accountable and contributing roles.
  • Uses examples rather than a vague quality score.
  • Calibrates subjective criteria and captures uncertainty.
  • Connects the release gate to user impact.
Treat benchmarks as shortlist evidence.

Choose a model on evidence

A frontier model scores higher on coding benchmarks. Should your research assistant switch?

Compare with an illustrative answer

Test the candidate against the current system on representative research tasks under equal tools and budgets. Measure evidence support, completeness, latency and cost per success. Include migration and reliability constraints, and keep the current version as a rollback option.

Review criteria

  • Explains why coding scores do not establish research quality.
  • Controls model, harness and budget differences.
  • Measures relevant task segments and costs.
  • Defines release and rollback criteria.
Trace the workload before changing prices.

Reduce cost without hiding failures

Your AI feature costs twice the forecast. What do you investigate first?

Compare with an illustrative answer

Inspect call counts, growing context, output/reasoning tokens, retries, cache misses and tool fees. Find the largest contributor. Test fewer sequential calls or shorter useful output against a quality floor; compare total cost per successful task, not token price alone.

Review criteria

  • Uses usage traces rather than guessing.
  • Includes retries and non-model costs.
  • Changes a measurable bottleneck.
  • Protects a stated quality floor.
Decompose the evidence path.

Debug a grounded answer

The assistant cites a policy page but gives the wrong refund rule. Is this a model problem?

Compare with an illustrative answer

Check document freshness and permissions, retrieval recall, ranking, selected passage and whether the claim is supported. Label the failing stage and add an eval. Retrieval changes may solve the problem without a new model or weight training.

Review criteria

  • Does not equate a citation with support.
  • Separates retrieval and generation errors.
  • Tests freshness and unanswerable queries.
  • Proposes a narrow experiment and regression case.
Show the decisions and evidence, not invented credentials.

Tell a credible transition story

You have PM experience but no model-training job. How do you demonstrate AI PM readiness?

Compare with an illustrative answer

Choose a real workflow you understand, document the baseline and users, and build a bounded prototype. Include an eval set, failure analysis, cost model, permissions and a pilot plan. Describe exactly what you did and what remains untested. Transfer domain judgment without claiming unsupported production impact.

Review criteria

  • Connects prior PM strengths to an AI product decision.
  • Includes inspectable artifacts.
  • Distinguishes prototype evidence from production results.
  • Explains a trade-off and a lesson from failure.
